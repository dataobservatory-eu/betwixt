<center><big>'''Hosiery and Footwear'''</big><br />exhibition in the [[Vikipeedia:GLAM/Traditional Livonian Clothing|Traditional Livonian Clothing]]</center>

This room of the virtual exhibition presents socks and footwear from the collections of the Finnish and Estonian national museums, characteristic of the Livonian Coast.
The earliest method of covering the feet across a wide region, including Livonian-inhabited areas, was wrapping them with footcloths and wearing woven bast shoes or simple one-piece leather. Footcloths were plain rectangular strips of fabric, usually torn from worn-out linen or woollen garments. However, because of their modest appearance, such cloths rarely made their way into museum collections.
From the Livonian Coast, museums preserve hand-knitted woollen socks that represent the patterned sock tradition of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. At this time, socks typically displayed brightly coloured striped ornaments. Longer socks (knee-length or above the knee) were usually part of women’s attire, while shorter socks were worn by both women and men. All these socks belonged to festive dress, as for everyday work people wore plain, unpatterned woollen socks in natural white or grey.

The simplest traditional peasant footwear were bast shoes and moccasins. Bast shoes could be woven from linden bark or the bark of other trees (with or without the outer layer). The coarser types, such as those shown here, were basic work and everyday shoes, typically worn over feet wrapped in footcloths. Slightly finer were the moccasins—shoes made from a single piece of leather with ties. In the early and mid-19th century, moccasins were still considered relatively fine footwear, even worn with wedding dress. By the end of the century, however, Livonians already wore leather shoes and boots with festive clothing, while moccasins remained as footwear for everyday work.
