<center><big>'''Handwear'''</big><br />exhibition in the [[Vikipeedia:GLAM/Traditional Livonian Clothing|Traditional Livonian Clothing]]</center>

This room of the virtual exhibition presents Livonian knitted mittens and gloves from the collections of the Finnish and Estonian national museums. Gloves and mittens were not only a practical garment accessory but also carried symbolic meaning. In cold weather, they were worn for warmth, and work handwear protected the hands from injury. Yet in this region, mittens and gloves also served as festive adornments—not always worn on the hands, but sometimes tucked behind a belt as part of dress. Up until the early 20th century, mittens and gloves were the most popular gift among peasants. They were given on festive occasions and as tokens of gratitude or goodwill. For example, a bride was expected to prepare several dozen pairs of mittens and gloves for her wedding, to be presented to her future husband’s family and relatives.

The mittens displayed in this exhibition are particularly ornate, with many of them featuring traditional multicoloured patterns that required considerable skill to knit. The dominant colours are red, white, and black. The gifting of such mittens on ceremonial occasions was widespread, and not all could even be worn out. As a result, they survived in larger numbers and later found their way into museum collections.

The knitted gloves represent a later stage of glove-making, namely the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when lace patterns were often incorporated. Such gloves were most frequently white. They also reflect an older tradition of knitting gloves with a brightly patterned cuff combined with a plain white palm and fingers.
